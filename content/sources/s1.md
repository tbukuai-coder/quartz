# s1: Simple Test-Time Scaling

> The simplest approach to test-time scaling: SFT on 1,000 curated reasoning traces + budget forcing (appending "Wait" to extend thinking) beats o1-preview on competition math by up to 27%.

## Key Contributions
- **s1K dataset**: Just 1,000 questions with reasoning traces, curated via three criteria — quality, difficulty, diversity — sufficient to unlock strong reasoning
- **Budget forcing**: A dead-simple technique to control test-time compute — suppress the end-of-thinking token and append "Wait" to force the model to continue reasoning, often self-correcting errors
- **Minimal recipe**: SFT on 1K examples for 26 minutes on 16 H100s → competitive with o1-preview
- **Test-time scaling demonstration**: Performance scales smoothly with more thinking tokens (budget forcing)

## Method
1. **Data curation** (s1K):
   - Collect 59K questions from 16 sources (math competitions, science olympiads, puzzles)
   - Filter by **quality** (correct solutions, good formatting)
   - Filter by **difficulty** (must require >10K thinking tokens from Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct)
   - Select for **diversity** via embedding-based clustering → final 1,000 samples
2. **Training**: Standard SFT on Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct with s1K. 26 minutes on 16×H100, learning rate 1e-5, sequence length 32K
3. **Budget forcing** (inference):
   - To **extend thinking**: When model generates end-of-thinking token, suppress it and append "Wait" — model continues reasoning, often catching its own errors
   - To **limit thinking**: Forcefully terminate after N tokens
   - Sequential scaling: each "Wait" can trigger deeper self-reflection

## Results
- **s1-32B exceeds o1-preview** by up to 27% on MATH and AIME24
- Budget forcing scales from 50% → 57% on AIME24 (extrapolates beyond no-intervention performance)
- With just 1K training examples — orders of magnitude less data than competitors
- Ablations show: quality and difficulty matter most; diversity helps but less
- 100 examples already capture >80% of the improvement; diminishing returns beyond 1K

## Datasets Used
- **s1K** (released) — 1,000 curated reasoning examples from math/science competitions
- Source pools: AIME, AMC, HMMT, Putnam, USAMO, science olympiads, puzzles, teasers

## Models Released
- **s1-32B** — Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct fine-tuned on s1K
